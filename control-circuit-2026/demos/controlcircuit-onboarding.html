⚡ Your First 5 Minutes: The Fast Track

Zero to Export
Step 1

New Project

Click File > New Project (Wizard). As a minimum just enter a Project Name, all other defaults will be good for an IEC spec. and A3 Landscape drawing sheets. In the wizard you can preset multiple aspects of your project including Client, Job #, wiring style mm² or AWG, etc. At any time visit Project > Properties to add or amend project-wide data fields, or even create your own.

Step 2

Drop Symbols

From the left-side palette, select Symbols Tab, then IEC Library, drag a Contactor or Breaker from the Tree or Preview Panel onto the 5mm grid. The grid is conditional - it can be re-sized, but never switched off, and 5mm is the perfect pitch for circuit wiring.

Step 3

Wire & Terminals

On the left-side palette, choose the Symbols Tab, select wire Nodes, place them around the outline of your intended circuit. Ensure all Node legs align orthogonally for auto-wire to work! Where a node's legs face the wrong way, select it and tap Alt+↑ ↓ ← → to spin it round on the spot; the plain arrow keys nudge it a grid step at a time. Tap Alt+W at any time to auto-wire. Alt+DEL to delete all wires. Alt+S to straighten any non-ortho wires. You may also draw wires between pin connection boxes. Later on, you may click a wire to assign Wire Group and auto-number Net Name - but we do not need that for simple sketches.

Step 4

Auto-Number & Preview

Run Tools > Renumber All to assign default Tags. You have complete control over Tag assignment, numbering method or even to set your own and lock them. Tags are used in many ways: a symbol with a Contact Role of MAIN becomes an element that hosts a look-up table for all sub-elements like NO, NC and AUX. Tags can be freely created, or perhaps =FUNC+LOC style - entirely up to you. Choose File > Print Preview for a default look at your first Circuit Sheet.

🔓 Read This First The Help Panel Follows You — Until You Lock It
Live mode 🔓 — help follows the mouse. Hover any menu item, toolbar button or tool and the Help panel changes to that topic on its own. You never have to go looking: point at the thing you do not recognise and read what it is. Press F1 at any time for the active tool.
Locked mode 🔒 — help stays put. Click the button in the top-right of the Help panel to toggle. Locked keeps the topic on screen while you work through it, so reading a set of instructions and then carrying them out does not replace the instructions the moment you reach for a menu.
The button tells you which you are in. It reads 🔓 Live or 🔒 Locked and turns red and bold when locked. If the panel has stopped following your mouse, that button is why — click it once.
The main window starts Locked. That is deliberate: there the Help panel shares a tab with the properties you are editing, and a panel that changed under you every time you crossed a menu would be unusable. Help opened in its own window starts Live.
TB-01 · Plan

Before You Begin Drafting

Three core design decisions that shape your workflow — from rough conceptual sketches to fully automated, high-granularity industrial packages including validation of every wire, connection and Terminal block. Go simple, or go deep - your call.

System Scope: Sketches to Full Automation

ControlCircuit flexes to how you work: draft a quick, unnumbered conceptual sketch in minutes, or build a multi-bay industrial control package. If hand-authoring device tags and wire numbers is your preference, you have full manual control. If you need complete automated sequencing, the background engines handle everything. Native support for =Function +Location prefixes.

Tip: Draw Location Boxes to automatically prefix all enclosed device tags.

Wire Groups: mm², AWG & Visual Styles

Wire Groups define physical wire gauges in both metric (mm²) and North American (AWG) standards, along with customizable colors, line thicknesses (e.g. heavy power buses vs thin signal wires), and diamond/slash numbering glyphs. These visual styles reflect accurately on screen and in all exported PDF, Web, and DXF drawings.

Tip: Configure project wire groups in Project Properties before routing.

Required Reports & Live Forms

Generate purchase-ready Bills of Materials, itemized Component Lists, Terminal Schedules, and Cable Plans. Forms extract directly from placed schematic instances. When lists grow, the built-in Shortfall Engine automatically prompts to generate overflow continuation sheets.

Tip: Tags in Component Lists become live clickable links in Web & Smart PDF!
TB-02 · Core Concepts

Four Foundational Primitives

The core object model powering ControlCircuit's intelligence, live cross-referencing, and automated reports.

1. Smart Symbols & Components

Placed components may carry manufacturer component data (library symbols are clean generic templates without predefined part data). Contact Cross-Reference matrices dynamically link coils to contact sets and auxiliary elements across sheets.

2. Terminal Groups & Nodes

A Terminal Group defines the terminal strip type (e.g. -X1); individual terminal instances represent physical slices with directional pin sites. Lightweight Terminal Nodes are also available, and both types can be freely mixed and matched in a circuit.

3. Wire Groups & Numbering

Enforces gauge standards (mm² and AWG), custom line thicknesses, display colors, and diamond/slash wire number glyphs (configurable for both render and print-out/export). Wire number nodes can be manually edited, or fully automated if Net Names are configured.

Later on you will find in your toolbox - Cable Markers and Shieldings, with these you can define a group of cables, assign multi-core numbers, and of course component data, even if cores are spread across the circuit.

4. Wire Nodes & Routing

Wire Nodes are smart - Angle, Branch, Connection and Interruption Point Nodes, like Pins on symbols are first-class citizens in Circuit. The engine has rules to prevent Nodes from being over-wired, or left unwired even. Later you will discover the Connectivity Engine and Net Runner tools that take all the guess-work out of circuit validation.

Build up a circuit with Nodes, then use the Free and Smart wire tools, or just hit Alt+W for fully automated wiring between ortho-aligned Nodes!

Project Properties

Configure project title, client info, standard wire group defaults, and auto-backup intervals.

03

Title & Index Sheets

Add the cover title sheet. Title block fields automatically sync from Project Properties.

04

Seed Report Forms

Insert seed pages for BOM, Component List, and Terminal Plans. They populate dynamically.

Whilst the included templates for automated Forms include all you need for Bill of Materials, Component Lists, Terminal Lists, etc., the separate Report menu offers the high-granularity of detailed reports for purchasing, wire and cable schedules, etc., for all that pre- and in-build activity. Exporting reports directly to PDF and CSV is included.

Forms are for that detail required by your customer's technicians for maintenance, diagnostics and part-finding.

05

Sheet & Folder Hierarchy

Organize drawing sections into logical folders (e.g. Power Distribution, PLC I/O, Safety, Report Forms), etc.

Use of folders is entirely optional and cosmetic, really depends on the scale of your project. However, note, for Web Publisher - Folders are fully supported - and make browser navigation a snap! Any Sheet not belonging to a folder is added after the last folder in the project tree. Sheets are automatically renumbered according to their position in the structure, after every update - so Folders are the way to keep your circuit, forms and lists in the order you require.

Whilst Sheets may be re-arranged outside and inside folders by drag action, for your convenience, hit Alt+O for The Organizer. It simplifies the structure management, allowing mass edits, updates and deletes.

06

User Preferences

From Tools > Personalize Workspace you may set your preferences, including what colors or canvas backgrounds are assigned for your 3 new friends - Drawing Layers! You have Schematic, Annotation and Rubber Stamp layers to make your presentation highly flexible. All circuit wiring goes on Schematic (F2), all Annotation (F3) is for all those 'workshop build' details and notes, required for assembly, not for customer final docs. And for your convenience - Rubber Stamp (Sheet 0) where you may add any comments, like "Draft", which are then applied on every sheet printed or exported (fully optional and configurable, of course.)

📦 Power Feature Reusable Circuit Typicals (.ccgrp)
Save Complete Sub-Circuits: Select any motor starter, safety circuit, or PLC rack and press Ctrl+G to group, then Format > Save Group to create a portable .ccgrp file with full vector preview.
Smart Landing & Tag Clash Resolution: Landing a group automatically checks for duplicate tags and prompts you to increment or retain tags intelligently. Press Ctrl+U to ungroup if needed.
🔄 Power Feature Smart Symbol Replacement & Pin Remapping
Right-Click Canvas Swap: Right-click any placed symbol on the canvas and choose Replace Selected Symbol... to swap it with any component from standard (IEC, NFPA), vendor, or custom user libraries without deleting or redrawing existing connections.
Smart 3-Tier Pin Auto-Matching:
• Tier 1 (Exact ID): Pairs identical pin labels instantly (e.g. A1➔A1, 13➔13).
• Tier 2 (Spatial Position): Matches pins occupying identical coordinates in symbol space (e.g. NO to NC contacts).
• Tier 3 (Positional Sequence): Aligns multi-pole contact flows top-to-bottom and left-to-right (breakers, contactors).
Zero-Friction Wire Re-anchoring & Tag Preservation: Connected wire endpoints dynamically glide to the new symbol's pin positions without breaking net connectivity. Component Tags (e.g. -10KM1), custom properties, and cross-references stay fully preserved.

TB-06 · Automation Engines Idempotent · Re-run anytime

The Automated Numbering Engines

Run project-wide or per-sheet. These engines are completely deterministic and safe to re-run at any stage.

🏷️ Symbol Renumbering

Automatically sequences device tags (e.g. -10KM1, -10Q1, -10F1) based on page coordinates or schematic flow.

🔢 Terminal Strip Runs

Sequences terminal numbers across strips (e.g. -X1:1..24), recognizing internal jumpers, multi-tier terminals, and field destinations.

⚡ Wire Numbering & Mass Update

Propagates wire numbers across nets. Press Alt+M for Mass Wire Update or Shift+F6 for Manual Wire Numbering Mode.

📑 Auto-Populate & Shortfall

Populates BOMs and Component Lists. If data exceeds one sheet, the Shortfall Engine automatically prompts to insert overflow continuation sheets.

🔗 Contact Cross-References

Instantly calculates contact matrix tables under relay/contactor coils, listing sheet and zone locations for all NO and NC auxiliary contacts.

🌐 Connectivity Engine & Net Runner

Press F5 for active sheet connectivity trace, Shift+F5 for project-wide trace, or F6 for Net Runner net diagnostics.

🔁 Interchange DXF In and Out — Including Symbols Drawn in Another CAD Package
No separate DXF menu. A single symbol goes out through File > Export Symbol and comes back through File > Import Symbol — just choose DXF as the file type. Whole sheets are a different job: use Publish > DXF Portfolio for those, which needs a Pro license. Pro
Draw a pin as a 5 mm stub. On layer CC_PINS, draw a 5 mm line from the connection point outward, in the direction the pin faces, with the pin's Id as text at the outer end. That is the entire convention — a pin facing left is a stub running left. 5 mm is the sheet grid pitch, so both ends land on the grid a placed symbol must sit on.
Layers carry the meaning. SCHEMATIC is the artwork, CC_PINS the pin stubs, CC_FIELDS the data fields (the text becomes the field name), and CC_BBOX a dashed reference box that is recomputed on import — edit or delete it freely.
The import asks one question. Plug, socket, screw or cage clamp is a property of the terminal, not of any geometry, so no drawing can express it. You are asked once and the answer applies to every pin in the file — and is remembered for the session, so a folder of symbols off one panel is a straight Enter after the first.
One rule: stay inside the extents. The export frames the file on the drawn geometry and the import finds those extents again to locate the origin. A stray line outside them moves the frame and shifts the whole symbol.
Interchange, not a save format. .ccsym stays canonical. A trip out to DXF and back is a redraw rather than a restore — genuine round trip, but some things return as what they became. The Symbol Editor's DXF Interchange help chapter lists exactly what changes.

TB-09 · Present & Review

Presentation Mode

Drive a design review from the live project instead of a printed set — press F11.

Everything you publish is already clickable: a tag jumps to its device, an index entry to its sheet, a contact table row to the contact it lists. Presentation Mode brings that same behaviour back into the editor. Both side panels collapse, the drawing takes the whole window, and the pack can be walked in front of a customer without exporting anything first.

Press F11 to enter, F11 or Esc to leave. The mouse vocabulary is deliberately small, and two of the four are unchanged from editing so nothing you already know moves under you:

GestureWhat it does
Left clickFollow whatever is under the pointer.
Right clickBack.
Middle dragPan, as in edit mode.
WheelZoom, as in edit mode.
← →Previous and next sheet, wrapping at the ends.
Home EndFirst and last sheet.

Nothing can be changed while you present

Every command that could edit, save or alter a sheet is greyed out for the duration, so the unsaved-work question is asked once, on the way in — never again between clicks. Printing, Print Preview, Page Setup, the Navigator, the reports and the exports all stay live. If someone asks for a print of what is on screen, Ctrl+P still does it and you carry on talking.

The pointer tells you what a click will do

A hand means the click will take you somewhere. A question mark means it will tell you something — that one appears over a device tag and opens its component card: quantity, part number, manufacturer, description and supplier for every item linked to the device. Size it N / M / L / XL; reach for XL when someone at the back asks for a part number.

Only links that actually resolve are offered, so a hand cursor is a promise the click will keep. Where you land is marked with four corner brackets that stay put while you talk about it — zooming in and panning around leave them alone, which is exactly when the room needs them most.

Set it up for the room

Tools > Personalize Workspace > Presentation sets the bar size, whether the sheet is fitted to the window on arrival, whether the menu bar and toolbars are hidden for full screen, and the colour of those arrival brackets. They are saved per user, not per project — they describe the screen you are presenting on, not the drawing.
